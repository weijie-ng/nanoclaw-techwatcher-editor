## What it is

Narrative for the first two thirds, then a widening into analysis and
implications for the last third. Drawn as a glass: a wide bowl of story
narrowing to the stem of the turn, then flaring into the base of consequence.

It is the standard shape for reconstructions and post-mortems, the "how it went
wrong" piece. The story is told once, in order, and only then is it explained.

## Use it when / do not use it when

Use it when you have a timeline you can actually reconstruct with sourcing:
an operation, a negotiation that collapsed, an evacuation, an outage, a
procurement failure. Use it when the sequence itself is the evidence, so that
by the time the analysis arrives the reader has already drawn half of it.

Do not use it when the timeline is thin or unsourced, since chronology with
gaps reads as evasion. Do not use it for breaking news, where readers need the
outcome first: that is `inverted-pyramid`. Do not use it when the reveal, not
the sequence, is the point, which is `delayed-reveal`.

## The skeleton

| Block | Job | Budget | Required |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cold open | The moment of maximum tension, in scene | 1 to 2 paragraphs | Yes |
| Orientation | Who these people are, what was at stake, dated | 1 to 2 paragraphs | Yes |
| Narrative | The sequence in order, dated, sourced, no analysis | 60 per cent of the piece | Yes |
| The turn | One paragraph that stops the story and steps back | 1 paragraph | Yes |
| Analysis | Why it happened: the decision, the incentive, the missing check | 2 to 4 paragraphs | Yes |
| Implications | What changes now, and for whom | 2 to 3 paragraphs | Yes |
| Accountability | Who has answered, who has not, what was asked | 1 to 2 paragraphs | Yes |
| Close | Forward-looking, on the record | 1 paragraph | Yes |

Keep analysis out of the narrative section. The discipline of the format is
that the story is allowed to be a story, with the reader's judgment suspended
until the turn.

## Worked example

```
COLD OPEN
At 04:12 on 3 October the duty officer at the maritime coordination centre in
Piraeus logged a call she had been told would never come: the tanker had
switched off its transponder inside the exclusion zone, and it was not
answering.

NARRATIVE (dated, in order)
The vessel had been flagged in April... In June the insurer withdrew... On
1 October the coordination centre's escalation protocol was amended...

THE TURN
Three separate systems saw the tanker that night. None of them was designed to
tell the others.

ANALYSIS
The gap was not technical. Under the 2023 protocol...

IMPLICATIONS
Twelve more vessels operate under the same flag arrangement...
```

## Rhythm and length

Total 1,200 to 3,000 words. The narrative section runs long paragraphs with
short sentences: momentum comes from sequence, not from clause length. The turn
is deliberately short, often a single sentence on its own line, because the
change of gear is the effect. The base runs shorter paragraphs and longer,
more qualified sentences.

Datestamp the narrative aggressively. A reconstruction without dates is an
assertion.

## Failure modes

- **It never widens.** The piece ends when the story ends, leaving a
  well-told anecdote with no argument. This is the failure the format is named
  for: a glass with no base.
- **Analysis leaking into the narrative.** "In what would prove a fatal error,
  the officer..." breaks the format. Let the reader reach it at the turn.
- **A chronology with holes.** If three hours are missing, say so in the piece.
  Silent gaps read as concealment.
- **The base is longer than the bowl.** Then it is an analysis piece with a
  long anecdote, and should be structured as one.
- **No accountability block.** Naming what you asked and who declined to answer
  is part of the format, not an optional courtesy.

## Citations

Both layers, as set out in [citations.md](citations.md): inline attribution in
the prose plus a numbered marker to a source list. Source list placement for
this format: End of the piece, after the close.

Every dated step in the narrative carries a marker. A reconstruction is only
as good as its timeline sourcing, and an uncited step reads as a guess. Where
the timeline has a gap, cite what establishes the gap.

## Self-check

- [ ] The narrative section contains no analysis or foreshadowing
- [ ] Every step in the timeline is dated and sourced
- [ ] Known gaps in the timeline are stated in the piece
- [ ] The turn is a distinct, short, unmissable paragraph
- [ ] The analysis explains cause, not just consequence
- [ ] Implications name who is affected and how
- [ ] The base is shorter than the narrative
- [ ] Every dated step in the narrative carries its own marker, and stated gaps are cited too
