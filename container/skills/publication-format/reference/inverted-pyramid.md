## What it is

Classic wire-service structure, as used by Reuters and AP. The most newsworthy
fact comes first, and everything after it descends in importance, so an editor
can cut from the bottom at any point and still have a complete, publishable
story.

It is one continuous narrative. Unlike Smart Brevity there are no labelled
modules: the reader is carried by paragraph order alone, and the ordering is
the entire discipline.

## Use it when / do not use it when

Use it for breaking news, official announcements, press releases being
rewritten as copy, court rulings, election results, incidents. Use it whenever
the piece may be truncated by someone else, whether an editor, a preview pane
or a reader who stops after two paragraphs.

Do not use it when the interest is in sequence rather than significance: a
reconstruction of how something went wrong wants `martini-glass`. Do not use it
for a trend with no single triggering event, which wants `anecdotal-lede`. Do
not use it when the reader needs the significance flagged rather than inferred,
which wants `smart-brevity`.

## The skeleton

| Block | Job | Budget | Required |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dateline | Place and date of filing | 1 line | Yes |
| Lede | Who did what, to whom, where, when, and why it counts | 1 sentence, 25 to 35 words | Yes |
| Second paragraph | The next most important fact, plus first attribution | 1 to 2 sentences | Yes |
| Supporting detail | Numbers, scope, mechanism, in descending importance | 3 to 6 paragraphs | Yes |
| Quote | A voice with standing, attributed in full on first use | 1 to 3 quotes, spread | Yes |
| Context | What preceded this, for a reader arriving cold | 1 to 2 paragraphs | Yes |
| Counterpoint | Denial, dissent or the disputed reading | 1 paragraph | Yes if it exists |
| Background tail | Long-running history, procedural detail, boilerplate | 1 to 3 paragraphs | Optional |

The test of correct ordering: cut everything after any paragraph and what is
left must still stand as a story. If cutting the last paragraph loses the
point, the piece is not a pyramid.

## Worked example

```
BRUSSELS, 14 March - The European Union adopted its nineteenth sanctions
package against Russia on Tuesday, listing 42 companies in three countries
that it said had routed dual-use electronics to Russian defence manufacturers.

The package is the first to name entities registered in an EU candidate
country, according to the Council decision published in the Official Journal.

Twenty-eight of the 42 listed firms are intermediaries rather than
manufacturers, Council figures show. Trade flows through the listed entities
reached EUR 1.2bn last year, up from EUR 340m in 2022.

"This is about the route, not the product," said Kaja Virtanen, the Council's
sanctions coordinator, at a briefing in Brussels.

The measure had been blocked for four weeks by two member states seeking
exemptions for existing energy contracts. Two such exemptions, running to
March next year, were granted in the final text.

Russia's foreign ministry called the package "commercially motivated" and said
it would respond. The listed candidate-country firms have not commented.

The EU has adopted sanctions packages against Russia at roughly three-month
intervals since February 2022.
```

## Rhythm and length

Total 300 to 600 words for a straight news story. Paragraphs of one to two
sentences, rarely three: wire copy breaks early and often, because narrow
columns and preview panes punish long blocks. Attribution appears in the first
two paragraphs and is repeated whenever a new claim comes from a new source.

Names get full title and identification on first use, surname alone after.

## Failure modes

- **A chronological lede.** "Talks began in January" is not a lede. The lede is
  the outcome; the chronology goes in the tail if at all.
- **Burying the number.** If a figure is the story, it belongs in the lede or
  the second paragraph, not the fifth.
- **A lede carrying four clauses.** One sentence, but not one sentence of 60
  words. Split the fourth clause into the second paragraph.
- **Unattributed claims in the middle.** Attribution weakens as the piece runs
  on and the writer tires. Every contested claim carries a source wherever it
  sits.
- **The kicker.** A satisfying closing line means the piece is not cuttable
  from the bottom, which means it is not this format.

## Citations

Both layers, as set out in [citations.md](citations.md): inline attribution in
the prose plus a numbered marker to a source list. Source list placement for
this format: End of the piece, after the background tail.

Wire copy carries its attribution unusually heavily in the prose, in the first
two paragraphs and again wherever a new claim comes from a new source. The
markers sit on top of that, not instead of it.

## Self-check

- [ ] Cutting after any paragraph leaves a complete story
- [ ] Lede is one sentence and carries the outcome, not the process
- [ ] Every paragraph is less important than the one above it
- [ ] Every contested claim is attributed where it appears
- [ ] No labelled section headings anywhere in the piece
- [ ] The last paragraph is the one you would miss least
- [ ] No closing flourish
- [ ] Attribution appears in the prose by the second paragraph, and the source list closes the piece
