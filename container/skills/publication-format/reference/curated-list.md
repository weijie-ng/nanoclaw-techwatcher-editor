## What it is

The linkblog model: Stratechery's linked list, Sunday reads, "5 things you
should know". No lead story, no hierarchy, no connective prose. A numbered or
bulleted list where each item is a headline, one or two sentences of summary,
and a source link.

The newsletter's voice comes entirely from selection. What you chose to include
and what you left out is the whole editorial act, and the format is honest
about that.

## Use it when / do not use it when

Use it when several things happened and none dominates, when the value you add
is filtering rather than synthesis, and when readers trust your judgment about
what is worth their time. Use it for weekend reading lists and for beats where
readers want breadth.

Do not use it when one story genuinely dominates, which is `top-story-digest`.
Do not use it when the items connect into an argument, which is
`narrated-digest`, and do not let it drift into one: the moment you write "and
relatedly" between two items, you have changed format mid-piece. Do not use it
for a single subject, since a list of five aspects of one thing is an article
in disguise.

## The skeleton

| Block | Job | Budget | Required |
|---|---|---|---|
| Title and date | What this is and when | 1 line | Yes |
| Framing line | What the list covers and the basis for selection | 1 sentence | Optional |
| Item headline | The claim or the story, not the source's own headline | 1 line | Yes |
| Item summary | What it says and why it earned a place | 1 to 2 sentences | Yes |
| Item source | Outlet, author where it matters, and the link | 1 line | Yes |
| Sign-off | One line | 1 line | Optional |

Items are equal. Same length, same structure, no item marked as more important.
Order can be thematic or arbitrary, but it must not imply a ranking you are not
prepared to defend.

## Worked example

```
# Five things worth reading on the eastern frontier
Week of 14 March. Everything here is primary reporting or a primary document.

1. **Customs brokers have become the enforcement layer.**
   Small border firms are now making the practical call on whether a shipment
   moves, with no legal department and no state support. The best piece yet on
   where sanctions policy actually meets a loading bay.
   Baltic Review, Marta Kask. [Link]

2. **The nineteenth package, in full.**
   The Council decision itself, 60 pages. Annex II is where the candidate-
   country listings sit, and it is shorter than the coverage suggests.
   Official Journal of the European Union. [Link]

3. **Strait transits fell 14 per cent in February.**
   Port authority monthly data, published without comment. The insurance
   repricing story that everyone has been asserting now has a number behind it.
   Regional Port Authority. [Link]
```

## Rhythm and length

Total 250 to 600 words. Between four and eight items; five is the natural
length and the reason "5 things" is a genre. Each item 40 to 70 words,
including the source line.

No paragraph anywhere in the piece belongs to more than one item. That is the
structural test.

## Failure modes

- **Connective prose appears.** "Which brings us to", "relatedly", "on a
  similar note". Each of these is the format failing. Delete them or commit to
  `narrated-digest`.
- **One item three times the length of the others.** It wanted to be a lead
  item, so the edition wanted `top-story-digest`.
- **Copying the source's headline.** Your headline states what you think is
  the point, which is the value you are adding.
- **Summaries that do not say why it was chosen.** Without that, the list is a
  feed, and the reader could have had the feed.
- **Unlinked items.** The link is not optional in a format whose purpose is
  pointing.

## Citations

Both layers, as set out in [citations.md](citations.md): inline attribution in
the prose plus a numbered marker to a source list. Source list placement for
this format: No separate source list. Each item's source line is its citation.

This is the one format exempt from numbered markers, because every item
already is a source and a second layer would duplicate the first. What the
exemption does not cover: any figure or claim you make in your own summary of
an item still needs attribution in that summary, naming where it came from.

## Self-check

- [ ] No lead item, no item visibly ranked above the others
- [ ] Item lengths are within about twenty words of each other
- [ ] No connective sentence anywhere between items
- [ ] Every item links on the source text itself, named
- [ ] Every summary says why the item earned its place
- [ ] Headlines are yours, not copied from the source
- [ ] Between four and eight items
- [ ] Every item names its source and links to it, and any figure in a summary says where it came from
