## What it is

The Axios AM/PM model, and the shape most morning briefs use. One story gets
the full Smart Brevity treatment at the top, and three to six shorter items
follow in a tighter form: headline, one line of what happened, one line of why
it matters, link.

The hierarchy is the product. The reader is being told not just what happened
but what mattered most, and the length of each item is the signal.

## Use it when / do not use it when

Use it for a recurring brief on a beat where one story usually dominates:
a daily or twice-weekly geopolitics or policy note. Use it when readers want
to be caught up in three minutes and will read the lead properly and skim the
rest.

Do not use it when nothing dominates. A forced lead item on a flat news day
misleads the reader about the shape of the week, and `curated-list` is honest
about it. Do not use it when the items are related by an argument rather than
by importance, which is `narrated-digest`.

## The skeleton

| Block | Job | Budget | Required |
|---|---|---|---|
| Masthead line | Edition, date, and a one-line read time | 1 line | Yes |
| Lead headline | Axiom-style, six words or fewer | 1 line | Yes |
| Lead lede | The most important sentence of the edition | 1 sentence | Yes |
| **Why it matters** | The consequence of the lead story | 1 to 2 sentences | Yes |
| **Driving the news** | The concrete development, sourced | 2 to 4 bullets | Yes |
| **What's next** | The next dated event on the lead story | 1 sentence | Yes if one exists |
| Digest items | 3 to 6 short items, each headline + what + why + link | 40 to 60 words each | Yes |
| Sign-off | One line: what to watch, or when the next edition lands | 1 line | Optional |

Digest items are all the same length. Varying them reintroduces a hierarchy the
reader cannot see the basis for. If one digest item wants to be longer, it
should have been the lead.

## Worked example

```
GEOPOLITICS BRIEF - Tuesday 14 March - 3 min read

## EU adds 42 firms to sanctions list

The nineteenth package names 42 companies in three countries accused of routing
dual-use electronics to Russian defence manufacturers.

**Why it matters:** It is the first package to list firms in an EU candidate
country, turning an enforcement measure into an accession problem.

**Driving the news:**
- Adopted Tuesday after four weeks of blocked votes.
- 28 of the 42 are intermediaries, not manufacturers (Council figures).
- Two energy-contract exemptions granted, running to March next year.

**What's next:** Member states report enforcement measures within 30 days.

---

**Strait transit falls to five-year low.** Transits through the strait fell
14 per cent in February, port authority data shows. *Why it matters:* Insurers
have begun repricing the route, which raises costs well beyond the parties to
the dispute. [Link]

**Summit communique drops climate finance line.** The final text omits the
2030 commitment present in every draft. *Why it matters:* The omission was
requested by two members whose votes were needed elsewhere. [Link]
```

## Rhythm and length

Total 400 to 700 words. Lead item 200 to 300, digest items 40 to 60 each. Whole
edition read in three minutes, and say so in the masthead line, because the
promise is part of the format.

A visible rule or clear break between the lead and the digest is required. The
reader needs to see the gear change.

## Failure modes

- **A manufactured lead.** Promoting a routine item to lead because the format
  demands one. If nothing dominates, switch format for that edition.
- **Digest items of uneven length.** Signals an importance ranking the reader
  cannot verify.
- **`Why it matters` on digest items restating the headline.** With one line to
  work with, a restatement wastes the entire item.
- **More than six digest items.** Past six, the reader is scanning, not
  reading, and `curated-list` or `thematic-clusters` serves them better.
- **Unlinked items.** A digest item without a source link is an assertion the
  reader cannot check.

## Citations

Both layers, as set out in [citations.md](citations.md): inline attribution in
the prose plus a numbered marker to a source list. Source list placement for
this format: End of the piece. Digest items keep their own inline links as
well.

The lead item is cited exactly as a `smart-brevity` piece would be, and every
digest item carries a marker of its own. One link per claim: the marker is the
link, so an item never also carries a bare link to the same source.

## Self-check

- [ ] The lead genuinely dominates the period covered
- [ ] The lead carries a real `Why it matters`, not a restatement
- [ ] Between three and six digest items
- [ ] Digest items are within ten words of each other in length
- [ ] Every digest item has its own distinct `why it matters`
- [ ] Every item links to a source
- [ ] The lead and the digest are visually separated
- [ ] The lead is fully marked up, every digest item links out, and figures in digest items carry markers
