## What it is

Axios house style. The piece is broken into short labelled blocks, each doing
one job, so a reader can take the headline and the first label and leave with
the point intact. It is modular by design: the blocks are readable out of
order, and most readers will not read them all.

The defining move is the **Axiom headline**, a six-word-or-fewer statement of
what happened, followed immediately by a one-sentence strong lede and then
`Why it matters`. The reader is told the significance before the detail.

## Use it when / do not use it when

Use it when the reader will act on the information, has limited time, and
wants the significance before the narrative. Briefings, policy notes,
announcements with consequences, anything a busy person reads on a phone
between meetings.

Do not use it when the material is a story rather than a development: if the
interest is in how something unfolded, the labels fight the narrative and you
want `martini-glass` or `anecdotal-lede`. Do not use it for a subject the
reader knows nothing about, where `Why it matters` lands before they know what
"it" is; use `explainer-faq`. Do not use it when you have only one fact, since
the format then has four blocks restating the same sentence.

## The skeleton

| Block | Job | Budget | Required |
|---|---|---|---|
| Axiom headline | State what happened, in the fewest words that stay accurate | 6 words or fewer | Yes |
| Strong lede | The single most important sentence in the piece | 1 short sentence | Yes |
| **Why it matters** | The consequence, not a restatement of the lede | 1 to 2 sentences | Yes |
| **Driving the news** | The concrete development: who did what, when, sourced | 2 to 4 bullets or 1 short paragraph | Yes |
| **By the numbers** | The figures that carry the story | 3 to 5 figures, each with a unit and a source | Optional |
| **The big picture** | Where this sits in the longer pattern | 2 to 3 sentences | Optional |
| **What they're saying** | One or two direct quotes, attributed | 1 to 2 quotes | Optional |
| **The other side** | The strongest contrary reading, stated fairly | 1 to 2 sentences | Optional |
| **What's next** | The next dated, checkable event | 1 to 2 sentences | Yes if one exists |
| Go deeper | A link out | 1 line | Optional |

Blocks appear in this order. Optional blocks are cut, never reordered, and
never filled with filler to look complete.

## Worked example

```
## EU adds 42 firms to sanctions list

The EU's nineteenth package names 42 companies in three countries accused of
routing dual-use goods to Russia.

**Why it matters:** For the first time the list includes firms in a country
that is formally an EU candidate, which turns an enforcement measure into an
accession problem.

**Driving the news:**
- The Council adopted the package on Tuesday after four weeks of blocked votes.
- 42 entities are listed, 28 of them intermediaries rather than manufacturers.
- Exemptions were granted for two energy contracts running to March.

**By the numbers:** EUR 1.2bn in listed trade flows last year, up from EUR 340m
in 2022, according to Council figures.

**What's next:** Member states have 30 days to report enforcement measures.
The Commission's review of candidate-country alignment is due in November.
```

## Rhythm and length

Total 250 to 450 words. Nothing beyond 700 without the format losing its
reason to exist. Sentences short, one idea each. Bullets are single-clause and
parallel in construction. Labels are bold, consistent, and always the standard
wording, because readers navigate by them.

White space between blocks is load-bearing. Do not run blocks together.

## Failure modes

- **`Why it matters` restates the lede.** The commonest failure. If it can be
  deleted without the reader losing anything, it was a restatement. It should
  answer "and therefore what", not "and to be clear".
- **Filling optional blocks to look complete.** A `By the numbers` with one
  number, or a `What's next` with "the situation will continue to develop",
  are both worse than an absent block.
- **Bullets that are really paragraphs.** If a bullet runs to two clauses and
  a subordinate, it belongs in the paragraph above.
- **An axiom headline that is a label, not a statement.** "Sanctions update" is
  a label. "EU adds 42 firms to sanctions list" is a statement.
- **Hedged lede.** The strong lede is the one sentence you would keep. Hedging
  it defeats the format.

## Citations

Both layers, as set out in [citations.md](citations.md): inline attribution in
the prose plus a numbered marker to a source list. Source list placement for
this format: After the `Go deeper` line, under a `Sources` heading.

Bullets under `Driving the news` each carry their own marker; a single marker
at the end of the block does not cover four separate claims. Figures in `By
the numbers` are the most-checked part of the piece and every one needs a
locator.

## Self-check

- [ ] Headline is six words or fewer and states what happened
- [ ] `Why it matters` says something the lede did not
- [ ] Every optional block present is carrying real content
- [ ] Every bullet is one clause and sourced
- [ ] Every figure has a unit and an attribution
- [ ] `What's next` names a dated, checkable event, or is absent
- [ ] Deleting any single block would cost the reader something
- [ ] Every bullet and every figure carries its own marker, and the source list follows `Go deeper`
