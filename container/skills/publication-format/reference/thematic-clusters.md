## What it is

Items grouped under two to four themed subheads rather than by chronology or
importance, with each cluster opening on a mini-intro sentence that says what
the cluster shows. "Sanctions", "Defence procurement", "Energy", each with two
or three items beneath it.

The point is pattern recognition. A reader who sees four sanctions items
together sees a direction of travel that the same four items scattered through
a list would not reveal.

## Use it when / do not use it when

Use it when you are covering one beat deeply and the items cluster naturally,
when the edition covers a longer period (a week or a fortnight) with enough
volume for grouping to mean something, and when you want the reader to notice
patterns rather than events.

Do not use it with fewer than about six items, since two clusters of two are
just a list with headings. Do not use it when the natural grouping is a single
theme, which means you have one story and should write an article. Do not
invent a cluster to house an orphan item: put it in the closest cluster or cut
it.

## The skeleton

| Block | Job | Budget | Required |
|---|---|---|---|
| Title and period | What beat, what dates | 1 line | Yes |
| Standfirst | The one thing to notice across all clusters | 1 to 2 sentences | Yes |
| Cluster heading | The theme, named as a noun phrase | 1 line | Yes |
| Cluster intro | What these items together show, stated as a claim | 1 sentence | Yes |
| Cluster items | 2 to 4 items: headline, one to two sentences, link | 40 to 60 words each | Yes |
| Next cluster | Repeat, 2 to 4 clusters total | - | Yes |
| Watch list | What would confirm or break the patterns named above | 2 to 3 bullets | Optional |

The cluster intro is what distinguishes this format from a list with subheads.
It must make a claim about the items, not announce them. "Three enforcement
actions, all aimed at intermediaries rather than manufacturers" is a claim.
"Sanctions news this week" is a heading repeated.

## Worked example

```
# The eastern frontier, 1 to 14 March
Enforcement is moving away from manufacturers and towards the people who move
the goods. Three of this fortnight's four clusters show it.

## Sanctions enforcement
All three actions this fortnight targeted intermediaries, not producers.

- **Nineteenth package lists 42 firms.** 28 of them are brokers or freight
  forwarders. First package to name candidate-country entities. [Link]
- **Two brokerages lose banking access.** Correspondent banks acted before any
  listing, which is the pattern to watch. [Link]

## Shipping and insurance
The commercial system is pricing the risk faster than any regulator is
imposing it.

- **Strait transits down 14 per cent.** Port authority data, no comment
  attached. [Link]
- **Two underwriters withdraw cover for the route.** [Link]

## Accession politics
The candidate-country listings have opened a second front.

- **Enlargement report brought forward to November.** [Link]

## Watch list
- Whether the 30-day enforcement reports name any intermediary.
- Whether a third underwriter withdraws.
```

## Rhythm and length

Total 500 to 900 words. Two to four clusters, two to four items each, six to
twelve items overall. Items are the same length within a cluster and across
clusters.

Cluster order carries meaning: lead with the cluster the standfirst is about.

## Failure modes

- **Cluster intros that only announce.** The single most common failure, and it
  reduces the format to a list with headings.
- **A cluster of one.** Either it belongs elsewhere or it is the lead of a
  `top-story-digest` edition.
- **Five or more clusters.** Past four, the grouping stops being a pattern and
  becomes a filing system.
- **Items filed by keyword rather than by meaning.** Two stories that both say
  "energy" but show nothing in common are not a cluster.
- **A standfirst that does not connect the clusters.** If nothing runs across
  them, the material may want `curated-list`.

## Citations

Both layers, as set out in [citations.md](citations.md): inline attribution in
the prose plus a numbered marker to a source list. Source list placement for
this format: End of the piece, after the watch list.

The cluster intro makes a claim about its items, so it is the writer's own
reading and carries no marker. The items beneath it carry theirs. Keeping that
line clear is what stops the intro borrowing authority from the sources below
it.

## Self-check

- [ ] Between two and four clusters
- [ ] Every cluster has at least two items
- [ ] Every cluster intro makes a claim about its items
- [ ] The standfirst names something visible across clusters
- [ ] Items are consistent in length within and across clusters
- [ ] No item was placed in a cluster by keyword alone
- [ ] Every item carries a marker to its source, and no second link beside it
- [ ] Cluster intros carry no markers, and every item beneath them does
