## What it is

The Benedict Evans model. One continuous voice moves across several stories
with real transitions and a point of view tying them together. It reads as a
single essay that happens to touch several stories, rather than as discrete
blocks.

It is the only newsletter skeleton here that builds an argument across items
instead of aggregating them. That is also why it is the hardest and the most
easily faked: the connections have to be real.

## Use it when / do not use it when

Use it when you have a thesis and the week's stories are evidence for it, when
your readers come for your reading of events rather than for coverage, and when
you can defend the argument if challenged.

Do not use it when the items do not actually connect. Forcing a thesis onto
unrelated stories produces the most annoying newsletter there is, and
`curated-list` is the honest alternative. Do not use it as a default voice
exercise. Do not use it for breaking news, where readers need facts before
interpretation.

## The skeleton

| Block | Job | Budget | Required |
|---|---|---|---|
| Title | The argument, not the period covered | 1 line | Yes |
| Opening claim | The thesis, stated plainly in the first two sentences | 1 paragraph | Yes |
| First story | The clearest evidence, told as evidence not as news | 2 to 3 paragraphs | Yes |
| Transition | A real connective: what the next story adds or complicates | 1 sentence | Yes |
| Second story | The complication, or the second axis | 2 to 3 paragraphs | Yes |
| Transition | As above | 1 sentence | Yes |
| Further stories | 1 to 3 more, each earning its place in the argument | 2 paragraphs each | Yes |
| Counter-reading | The strongest case that the thesis is wrong | 1 paragraph | Yes |
| Synthesis | What the argument amounts to, and what would disprove it | 1 to 2 paragraphs | Yes |
| Sources | The links, gathered at the end rather than inline | List | Optional |

Transitions are the format. A transition that only says "meanwhile" or
"elsewhere" is a paragraph break wearing a costume. Each one should state the
relationship: this confirms it, this complicates it, this is the same mechanism
in a different sector.

## Worked example

```
# Enforcement is moving down the supply chain

The interesting thing about this fortnight is not that the EU sanctioned more
companies. It is who it sanctioned. Two thirds of the new listings are
intermediaries: brokers, forwarders, ship managers. Nobody who makes anything.

Start with the package itself. Of the 42 firms named on Tuesday, 28 move goods
rather than produce them... The stated rationale is about routes, not products,
and the Council's own coordinator said so.

That would be a policy choice on its own. What makes it a direction of travel
is that the commercial system got there first. Two underwriters withdrew cover
for the strait route in February, before any listing...

The counter-reading is that this is capacity, not strategy: intermediaries are
listed because they are easier to identify than end users, and the shift says
more about what enforcement can see than about what it intends. That would
predict a return to manufacturer listings once the intelligence improves, and
it is the thing to watch in the next package.

What all this adds up to: the compliance burden is being pushed onto firms with
five employees and no legal department, and the enforcement question for the
next year is whether they can carry it.
```

## Rhythm and length

Total 800 to 1,600 words. Paragraphs longer than in any other format here, four
to six sentences, because the argument needs room to develop. First person is
permitted and usual. Voice is the product.

Stories are unequal in length by design, weighted by how much they carry for
the argument, which is the opposite of `curated-list`.

## Failure modes

- **Fake transitions.** "Meanwhile", "in other news", "speaking of which". If
  the connective does not state a relationship, the piece is a list pretending.
- **A thesis the stories do not support.** Readers notice. State the thesis
  after choosing the stories, not before.
- **No counter-reading.** An argument that never considers being wrong reads as
  a column, not analysis, and the counter-reading is usually the most useful
  paragraph in the piece.
- **Summarising instead of arguing.** If each section could be lifted out and
  put in a `curated-list` unchanged, nothing is being built.
- **Too many stories.** Past five the argument thins. Cut the weakest evidence.

## Citations

Both layers, as set out in [citations.md](citations.md): inline attribution in
the prose plus a numbered marker to a source list. Source list placement for
this format: End of the piece, under `Sources`.

This format runs on the writer's argument, so the line between sourced fact
and the writer's reading has to be visible sentence by sentence. Facts carry
markers; the argument connecting them carries none and says so in the prose.
The counter-reading is the writer's too, and is not cited.

## Self-check

- [ ] The thesis is stated in the first paragraph
- [ ] Every transition states a relationship, not just a change of subject
- [ ] Every story included is evidence for or against the thesis
- [ ] There is an explicit counter-reading
- [ ] The synthesis names what would disprove the argument
- [ ] No section could be lifted out unchanged into a flat list
- [ ] Five stories or fewer
- [ ] Facts carry markers, the argument and counter-reading visibly do not, and the two are never in the same register
