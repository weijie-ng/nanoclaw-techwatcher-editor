3 min read · 6 items

EU adds 42 firms to sanctions list

The nineteenth package names 42 companies in three countries accused of routing dual-use electronics to Russian defence manufacturers [1].

Why it matters: It is the first package to list firms in an EU candidate country [1], which turns an enforcement measure into an accession problem the Commission has to answer for in November.

Driving the news:

  • Adopted Tuesday after four weeks of blocked votes [2].
  • 28 of the 42 are intermediaries, not manufacturers, per Council figures [1].
  • Two energy-contract exemptions granted, running to March next year [1].

What's next: Member states report enforcement measures within 30 days, so by 13 April [1].

Strait transits fall to five-year low. Transits through the strait fell 14 per cent in February [3], port authority data shows, with no commentary attached. Why it matters: Insurers have begun repricing the route, which raises costs for shippers who are not party to the dispute at all.

Summit communique drops climate finance line. The final text omits the 2030 commitment that appeared in all four circulated drafts [4]. Why it matters: The omission was requested by two members whose votes were needed on an unrelated security annex.

Two brokerages lose correspondent banking. Both firms were cut off by their correspondent banks before any listing, according to people familiar with the decisions [5]. Why it matters: Banks are now acting ahead of regulators, which makes delisting almost irrelevant as a remedy.

Enlargement report brought forward to November. The Commission moved the publication date by four months without giving a reason [6]. Why it matters: It now lands before the next sanctions package, not after, which changes who is negotiating with what.

Third underwriter withdraws route cover. Cover was withdrawn for voyages transiting the exclusion zone, effective 1 April, per a broker's circular [7]. Why it matters: Two withdrawals were a commercial judgment; three is a market closing.

Next edition Thursday. Watching: whether any 30-day enforcement report names an intermediary.
