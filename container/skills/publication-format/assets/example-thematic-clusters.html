Fortnightly · 1 to 14 March

The eastern frontier, 1 to 14 March

Enforcement is moving away from the firms that make things and towards the firms that move them. Three of this fortnight's four clusters show it happening from a different direction.

Sanctions enforcement

All three actions targeted intermediaries rather than producers, and two of them happened before any formal listing.

Nineteenth package lists 42 firms. Twenty-eight are brokers or forwarders. First package to name candidate-country entities [1].

Two brokerages lose correspondent banking. Both cut off by their banks weeks before the package, according to people familiar with the decisions [2].

Enforcement review cited in three national cases. Prosecutors in two states are now leaning on January's Commission finding directly [3].

Shipping and insurance

The commercial system is pricing this risk faster than any regulator is imposing it, and is not waiting to be asked.

Strait transits down 14 per cent. Port authority monthly data, published without comment or explanation [4].

Third underwriter withdraws route cover. Effective 1 April, per a broker's circular [5]. Two was a judgment; three is a market closing.

Accession politics

The candidate-country listings have opened a second front that nobody involved seems to have planned for.

Enlargement report brought forward to November. Moved by four months, without a stated reason [6].

Two member states seek a carve-out mechanism. They want listings of candidate-country entities routed through a separate vote [7].

What would confirm this

  • Whether any 30-day enforcement report names an intermediary rather than a producer.
  • Whether a fourth underwriter withdraws before the June package.
  • Whether the carve-out mechanism reaches a Council agenda at all.
