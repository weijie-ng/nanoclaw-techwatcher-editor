EU adds 42 firms to sanctions list

Brussels · 14 March · 2 min read

The EU's nineteenth sanctions package names 42 companies in three countries accused of routing dual-use electronics to Russian defence manufacturers [1].

Why it matters: For the first time the list includes firms registered in an EU candidate country [1], which turns an enforcement measure into an accession problem the Commission now has to answer for in November.

Driving the news:

  • The Council adopted the package on Tuesday after four weeks of blocked votes [1].
  • 28 of the 42 listed entities are intermediaries rather than manufacturers, per Council figures [1].
  • Two energy-contract exemptions were granted, both running to March next year [1].

By the numbers: EUR 1.2bn in trade flowed through the listed entities last year, up from EUR 340m in 2022, on the Council's own estimate [2]. Nine of the 42 firms were incorporated after February 2022 [1].

The big picture: Enforcement has moved steadily away from producers and towards the firms that move goods. Two thirds of listings across the past three packages have been brokers, forwarders or ship managers [3].

What they're saying: “This is about the route, not the product,” said Kaja Virtanen, the Council's sanctions coordinator, at a briefing in Brussels [4].

The other side: Two member states argue the candidate-country listings pre-empt an accession judgment that is not the Council's to make, and blocked the package on that basis before securing the energy exemptions [5].

What's next: Member states have 30 days to report enforcement measures [1]. The Commission's enlargement report, brought forward to November, will address candidate-country alignment directly [6].
