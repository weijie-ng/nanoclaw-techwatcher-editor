EU sanctions 42 firms over dual-use electronics routing

By the Brussels bureau

BRUSSELS, 14 March – The European Union adopted its nineteenth sanctions package against Russia on Tuesday, listing 42 companies in three countries that it said had routed dual-use electronics to Russian defence manufacturers [1].

The package is the first to name entities registered in an EU candidate country, according to the Council decision published in the Official Journal [1].

Twenty-eight of the 42 listed firms are intermediaries rather than manufacturers, Council figures show [1]. Trade flows through the listed entities reached EUR 1.2bn last year, up from EUR 340m in 2022, on the Council's own estimate [2].

Nine of the companies were incorporated after February 2022. Three share a registered address in a single office building in Limassol, the decision states [1].

“This is about the route, not the product,” said Kaja Virtanen, the Council's sanctions coordinator, at a briefing in Brussels [3]. She said enforcement had shifted towards intermediaries because “that is where the decision to move a shipment is actually taken” [3].

The measure had been blocked for four weeks by two member states seeking exemptions for existing energy contracts [4]. Two such exemptions, running to March next year, were granted in the final text [1].

Russia's foreign ministry called the package “commercially motivated” and said it would respond [5]. Two of the listed candidate-country firms did not respond to requests for comment; a third said it had never handled controlled goods.

The listings follow an enforcement review published in January which found that controlled items were reaching Russian buyers through third countries in volumes the Commission described as “materially larger than previously assessed” [6].

The EU has adopted sanctions packages against Russia at roughly three-month intervals since February 2022. Member states have 30 days to report national enforcement measures to the Commission [1].
